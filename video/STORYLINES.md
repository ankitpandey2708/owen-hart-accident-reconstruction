# Storylines: Owen Hart accident (internal safety study)

**The one thing:** a single quick-release part was the only thing holding a person 78 ft in the air, and nothing backed it up.
**To remember:** a life-safety rig never has a single point of failure.

## A. One part, 78 feet (recommended)
- **Device / form:** the object is the explanation, filmed as one continuous take.
- **What the viewer sees and feels:** the film opens on the snap shackle itself, extremely close. The camera never cuts: it pulls back through the harness, up the cable to the rafters, down the 78 ft drop to the ring, and back out to a corrected rig. The viewer understands the failure because they watch the part open.
- **First 2 s:** macro of a steel snap shackle latching shut on a cable with a hard metallic click. The line "One part. 78 feet." prints beside it.
- **Beats:** shackle (hook) → the planned comedy descent → the lanyard tug that opens it → the fall in real time (2.2 s, 48 mph land as numbers) → slow replay to the top rope → one line of aftermath → the same camera finds a redundant rig → end line.
- **Look:** a night arena of black steel under amber ring light, with one red accent used only for the failure. Condensed sans type sits on the objects it names, with leader lines.
- **Sound:** low drone; cable creak; the shackle's click and snap; whoosh; one bass impact, then silence (the music drops out); a quiet tone returns for the fix.

## B. 2.17 seconds
- **Device / form:** a number lands, in one frame that rebuilds.
- **What the viewer sees and feels:** a single number, 2.17, dominates the screen and the whole film rearranges around it: the height becomes a ruler, the ruler becomes the fall, the fall becomes the clock of the evening (7:59 PM, 8:12 PM) and then the years after. It's sober and quiet.
- **First 2 s:** giant digits tick 0.00 → 2.17 over a silhouette of the ring, then stop.
- **Beats:** the number → what it measures (the fall) → why (the release) → the impact → the evening's clock → the settlement → the fix → end line.
- **Look:** a monochrome stage with large tabular numerals; amber for measurements and red for the moment of failure.
- **Sound:** a ticking pulse that stops at impact; sparse piano-like tones; silence under the timeline.

## C. Case file (bold)
- **Device / form:** an incident investigation, as a flight across one evidence board.
- **What the viewer sees and feels:** the camera travels across an investigator's blueprint board: Exhibit A (the shackle), the rig drawing, the reconstruction footage pinned in place, and a fault tree that grows one cause at a time until it reaches the corrective actions. It plays as a formal root-cause analysis, which suits an internal study.
- **First 2 s:** a tag stamps onto the shackle reading "EXHIBIT A: quick-release snap shackle, not rated for life safety".
- **Beats:** incident summary → Exhibit A → the rig as drawn → reconstruction clip → fault tree (hardware, release, no backup line) → corrective actions → end line.
- **Look:** engineering blueprint, cyan line work on deep navy, with red only on the failed branch.
- **Sound:** a low room tone, the stamp, the pencil and line draws, a reel-to-reel hum under the reconstruction, and the impact muted to a single thud.
